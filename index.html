Loom

A two-track effects chain for the NLM Xenomorpher. It also runs on standard O.R.N.8 hardware. Patch in your voices, drums or anything else from the rack: each track takes a sound, threads it through four effects and an output stage, and moves it with modulators and a clock. This manual covers Loom Beta 1, build c5cbadd8.

The weaving words

Warp and weft are the two sets of threads in woven cloth. The warp threads are stretched lengthwise on the loom and held still, under tension. The weft is drawn across them, over and under, from side to side. The warp carries the tension, and the weft carries almost none. The names are old: weft comes from the Old English wefan, “to weave”, and warp from weorpan, “to throw”. The thrums are the loose warp ends left on the loom when the cloth is cut off; the shuttle carries the weft back and forth. In everyday speech, the “warp and weft” of something is what it is made of.

Loom names its parts after the loom. Each track is a thread of sound, and the devices wind it, carry it, stretch it and weave it into cloth. Once you know what the words mean on a real loom, you can guess what the controls do:

Overview

Loom runs on the NLM Xenomorpher and on standard O.R.N.8 hardware. The controls, screens and jacks described here are the same on both.

Loom needs a Teensy 4.1 with PSRAM (the extra memory chips). 8 MB (the usual Teensy 4.1 setup) is enough: REPEAT, FIBRE and DRAFT take their fixed share, and loops and samples share the rest, each taking only the length it really holds, up to 16 s. Without PSRAM, SPOOL, SHUTTLE, FIBRE, DRAFT and REPEAT can't run. The boot screen shows the version, ALPHA 1, or a blinking NO PSRAM! when there is none. The build number is on the card mode page.

Loom has two tracks, A and B. Each track is a chain of six slots, played left to right as in the weave view. Each slot holds one of the devices stacked in its column:

Each track also has four modulators of its own (A–D), and both tracks share four more (E–H). Any setting can be moved by up to two of them. The eight CV jacks can output any modulator, so Loom can also drive the rest of your system.

In the rack. The two audio inputs take any line or modular level: an oscillator after its VCA, a drum module, a mixer's send. Envelopes and gates shape what Loom does with it. A sharp attack makes BEATER and REED ring, a sequence with rests leaves room for the echoes and grains, and the FOLLOW modulator turns the level coming in into movement. The trigger inputs, and the CV inputs used as gates, can press any device's actions (freeze, hit, record), and can run the clock from your sequencer. The eight CV outputs send the modulators and the clock back out to the rest of the rack.

The weave view

The weave view is Loom's home screen. It shows both tracks as two rows of boxes, each box one device in the chain, in the order the sound passes through them. Every box draws a small live picture of what its device is doing: a loop going round, a file playing, the level coming in. So you see the whole set at a glance.

Here you build the chain: put devices in the slots, change their order, bypass them, and open their pages. The bottom line names the device under the cursor and shows the level coming in.

How to play it

  • Turn R to move along track A, then on to track B.
  • Turn L to put another device in the slot. On CLOTH it sets the output level.
  • Push R to open the device's page. Y brings you back.
  • Hold R and turn on an effect to move it along the chain, for example REPEAT before TEXTURE.
  • Press B to bypass the slot. On SHUTTLE, press A to start it and hold A for 2 s to stop it.
  • Hold Y for 2 s to save everything (SAVE STATE).

  • R push opens the device's page.

When the processor is full

Loom can't run every effect on both tracks with every setting turned up at once. It looks after this itself, so the sound never breaks up:

  • CPU FULL. Before an effect comes in (B on a bypassed slot, a device picked with L, BEATER's sympathetic models, linking A and B to TIGHT), Loom checks that it still has room for it with all its settings turned up. If not, nothing changes and CPU FULL shows for a moment. Bypass or change another effect to make room. Anything Loom lets in can be turned up all the way afterwards.
  • Lighter when busy. When the load goes over 85 %, the heavy effects work lighter until it has been under 65 % for 3 s: REPEAT's reverb runs on 4 of its 8 lines, REED leaves out bands that are barely heard, TWILL lets ringing notes stop sooner and FIBRE makes at most 5 grains at a time.
  • Last resort. If the processor is ever completely swamped, Loom leaves out a few milliseconds of sound instead of freezing.

Linking A and B

The two tracks can run on their own or be linked. In THREAD, track A runs on into track B, one long chain over two rows. In LOOSE and TIGHT, the tracks keep their own sound but share the knobs, so a stereo pair or a doubled part moves as one.

A short label pops up when you change the link, and a small sign between the A and B letters on the left shows the mode afterwards. Nothing is lost on the way: B's own settings wait while it follows A, and come back when you leave.

How to play it

  • Press A+B together to step through FREE, THREAD, LOOSE and TIGHT.
  • In THREAD, put effects on track B to process track A further.
  • In LOOSE, set B a little different from A first: the offsets stay as you turn.
  • In TIGHT, B copies A exactly, both in the centre, one row to edit.

Device pages

Every device has a page of its own, and every page works the same way. The top line names the track and the device and shows its two actions. The middle is a live picture of what the device is doing to the sound. At the bottom sit four settings, names above and values below, on one or two pages.

Each setting has two sides. The R knob sets its value. The L knob sets its mode: another way of working, a zoom for the picture, or a variation that moves the setting around your value with every beat, so the sound keeps changing on its own.

How to play it

  • Turn R to move between the settings, and on to page 2.
  • Push R to grab a setting, turn R to change it, push R again to let go. Turn slowly for single steps, spin faster to cover a wide range quickly.
  • Turn L right for the setting's mode or variation; turn it back all the way left and its name returns.
  • Push R twice quickly to put a setting and its mode back to their defaults, with its name on top. Again, and everything comes back.
  • Press A and B for the device's actions. On an effect, B bypasses it.
  • Hold L and let go for the trigger page: a gate, CV input, MIDI note or modulator that presses the actions for you.
  • Hold R for 2 s to reset the whole device. Hold L for 2 s right after to undo it.
  • Hold Y for 2 s to save just this device: the rest stays as it was last saved.

Modulating a setting

Any setting on any page can be moved by up to two modulators at once: a wave, an envelope, a random pattern, a follower, a drum pattern, a clock divider, or a MIDI controller. They add up around the value you set, so the setting is the middle and the modulators swing it from there.

A modulated setting shows how far it can move as a line under its value, with a tick where it is right now, so you can see the movement even without hearing it. Pitch settings can also follow the notes of a MIDI keyboard.

How to play it

  • Put the cursor on a setting and push L: MOD1 shows at the top.
  • Turn L to pick the modulator, A to H, moving the setting one way (u) or both ways (b).
  • Turn R to set how much, up to ±100 %.
  • Push R for MOD2, a second modulator on the same setting.
  • Push L again to leave.
  • In MOD1, hold X to learn a knob from your MIDI controller.
More about Modulating a setting
  1. Put the cursor on a setting and push L. The top line shows MOD1.
  2. L turn picks the modulator (A–H) and whether it moves the setting one way (u) or both ways (b). From off, the first click to the right goes straight to the modulator you adjusted last, as u or b, whichever you picked last time. A fast turn stops there too. Turn on from there for the others.
  3. R turn sets the amount, from −100 % to +100 %.
  4. R push switches to MOD2, a second modulator for the same setting.
  5. Push L again to leave.

A modulated setting shows how far it can move as a line under its value, with a moving dot where it is right now. In MOD1, hold X to MIDI-learn the setting to a controller.

“The modulator you adjusted last” is the one whose page you last turned a knob on, or whose type you last changed in the MOD view. Loom remembers it, and its u or b, with the saved settings.

Playing a pitch from MIDI

Every pitch setting (FIBRE's, REED's and DRAFT's PITCH, BEATER's NOTE) has one more source: MIDI. In MOD1 or MOD2, turn the L knob one click left from off: it reads MIDI, and the amount goes to +100 % by itself. The setting now follows the last MIDI note played, on any channel: C3 (note 60) leaves it as it is, each key above or below moves it a semitone. At −100 % the keyboard runs upside down. The last note stays when you let go. Turn right again to get back to off.

With a RANDOM on MOD1 and MIDI on MOD2, the random melody is transposed by the key you hold.

INPUT

INPUT is the plain way in: the sound at the track's audio jacks, straight into the chain. It takes any line or modular level, in stereo or from one side only, and on track B it can also take track A's output, so B can process A without linking the tracks.

Its picture is a small oscilloscope. It shows the sound as it arrives, from a few cycles of a single note up to whole phrases, so you can see at a glance that something is coming in and how hard.

How to play it

  • Turn SOURC to pick the jacks: both, left, right, or off. On track B, OUT A takes track A's output.
  • Turn GAIN until the level box moves well but CLIP never shows.
  • Turn WIDTH down for mono, past 100 to spread a stereo source wider.
  • Turn VIEW short to see the shape of a wave, long to see rhythm and how each hit dies away.
  • Press A (MUTE) to fade the input out and back in. Press B (DUCK) for a quick dip, like a kick pumping it: give it a gate on the trigger page to pump in time.

SPOOL

SPOOL is a live looper at the start of the chain. It records what comes in, up to 16 seconds, and plays it round and round while you overdub, trim it, slow it down or turn it backwards. Everything after it in the chain works on the loop, so a phrase you play once becomes material for the effects to chew on.

It only holds memory for what you record: the loop keeps just its own length and gives the rest back. A loop can be saved to the card and opened in SHUTTLE, and a WARP on the other track picks it up by itself, so you can play it from a keyboard straight away.

How to play it

  • Press A to record. Press B to close the loop and play it.
  • Press A on a playing loop to overdub, and again to go back to just playing. MON's L knob (DUB) sets how much of the old loop stays.
  • Press B to stop and start the loop. Hold B for 3 s to clear it.
  • Pull in START and END to loop just a part. Turn their L knob to zoom in on the edge you move.
  • Turn SPEED for slower, faster or backwards (push R twice quickly to reverse it).
  • Hold A for 2 s to save the loop to the card, in LOOM/SPOOLS.
More about SPOOL

The first REC puts every setting except IN back to its default, so a new loop starts clean. The next A press closes the loop and carries on overdubbing; after that A switches between overdubbing and just playing. B while recording closes the loop and plays. While recording, the picture grows from the left, 4 s across at first and zooming out whenever it reaches the edge. If memory is short (the other track is recording too), the loop may stop earlier, and "memory full" shows when nothing is left.

A saved loop goes to LOOM/SPOOLS named by session, for example Spool_S014_01 (see file names), together with its settings (START, END, FADE, LEVEL, SPEED). SHUTTLE's file stays as it is, but the next time you open SHUTTLE's file list, it opens in SPOOLS with the cursor on the new loop: push R to load it, and it plays the way you set it up.

SHUTTLE

SHUTTLE plays sound files from the SD card: drum loops, field recordings, whole songs, your own saved loops and takes. It can stream any length straight from the card, or load a loop of up to 16 seconds into memory, where it can also play backwards.

It finds the tempo of a file by itself, so it can play in time with the clock, and each file remembers its own loop points and settings. Pick a file again weeks later and it comes back the way you left it.

How to play it

  • Push R on MODE to open the file list. Turn either knob to scroll, push R to open a folder or load a file, press Y to close the list.
  • In the list, press B for a quick listen, and hold B for AUTO: every file the cursor stops on plays by itself.
  • Press A to play, B (TOP) to go back to the start.
  • Turn SPEED's L knob to SYNC to play at the clock's tempo. SPEED then sets half time, double time and so on.
  • Pull in START and END for a loop, add FADE to smooth the seam, and set SNAP to BEAT to keep the loop whole beats long.
More about SHUTTLE

In the file list, “..” at the top goes up a folder. The quick listen plays the file under the cursor once, straight from the card and past all effects, so you can listen through a folder without changing what SHUTTLE plays. Loops in SPOOLS play just their loop section, as set in SPOOL; other files play whole. B again stops it, and so does closing the list. A small arrow marks the file you are hearing. Hold B again, or close the list, to turn AUTO off.

The top line shows the file's tempo while SHUTTLE is stopped, and the position in the file (minutes, seconds, tenths) while it plays, so pressing A switches between them. The tempo is found when the file loads, between 70 and 180 BPM. A streamed file (STRM) is read for about 30 s from the loop's START. A file in memory (MEM) is read as the loop it is, START to END and round again, so loops down to about 1.5 s get a tempo too. It is shown as you hear it, so SPEED changes it. The number is in BPM; - means no clear beat was found.

Each file keeps its own settings. When you pick a file, it comes back with the loop points, LOOP, LEVEL, SPEED, FADE and SNAP you last used with it. A file you have never used starts with the whole file as the loop: START at its beginning, END at its end. WARP

WARP turns a sound into an instrument. It plays the other track's loop or file, or a single-cycle waveform, at the pitch of the MIDI notes you play, up to eight notes at once. Middle C plays the sound as it was recorded; every key above or below plays it faster and higher or slower and lower, like a tape at another speed, and a held note goes round the sound for as long as you hold the key.

Each note has its own envelope, and pressing a key harder makes it waver, like a violinist's vibrato. So a drum loop becomes a pitched texture, a recorded voice a choir, and a waveform from the card a plain synth voice.

How to play it

  • Turn SRC's L knob to pick the source: SPL the other track's SPOOL loop, SHU its SHUTTLE file, THR a waveform from the card.
  • On THR, push R and turn to step through the waves. Push R twice quickly for the file list; the wave under the cursor plays at once.
  • Play notes from a MIDI keyboard or sequencer, on any channel.
  • On page 2, shape each note with ATK, DEC, SUS and REL. Their L knobs bend the curves.
  • Turn VEL up and soft keys play quieter than hard ones.
  • Press a key hard for vibrato. WEFT sets its speed, and its L knob what wavers: pitch, level, tone or pan.
  • Press A (TRIG) to play the sound at its own pitch, B (STOP) to let every note go.
More about WARP

Any quiet at the start and end of the sound is left out, so notes speak at once; the left-out parts are drawn dotted. A ninth note takes over the oldest one, fading it out quickly so nothing clicks. A note at full velocity is as loud as the sound was recorded. On the weave view WARP shows as WRP.

Where the sound comes from. On SRC, the L knob picks one of three sources:

  • SPL: the other track's SPOOL loop. Each time a recording or an overdub there ends, WARP takes the new loop over by itself, and notes that are still sounding play on into it. Record a loop on track A, then play it from a keyboard on track B.
  • SHU: the other track's SHUTTLE file, the loop section you set there (up to 16 s). It is read from the card once you stop moving the loop points.
  • THR: a thrum, a single-cycle waveform from the LOOM/THRUMS folder (see below). A fresh WARP starts here, on a random one.

Browsing thrums. With SRC on THR, push R once to grab it, then turn R: it steps through the waves in the thrum's folder, one by one, A to Z. Push R on a wave or press Y to close the list; the wave you heard last stays.

WEFT: pressing harder

WARP stretches a sound across the keyboard like warp threads on a loom (see the weaving words): every key holds it at another pitch. WEFT is what your hand does while a key is down, the thread drawn across. Press a key hard and its note starts to waver, like a violinist's vibrato: nothing happens up to three quarters of the key's pressure, then the wobble grows with the pressure, up to a semitone either way when you press all the way down. The L knob on WEFT picks what wavers instead of the pitch: LVL pulses the level, down to silence at full pressure, a tremolo; CUT sweeps a filter, darker the harder you press, with a wah-like wobble; PAN swings the note between left and right. It eases in and out, and every note wobbles at its own slightly different pace, so a chord shimmers rather than moving in lockstep. WEFT sets how fast. Each note follows its own key's pressure (polyphonic aftertouch) or the whole keyboard's (channel aftertouch), whichever is pressed harder; a keyboard without aftertouch just plays the notes plainly.

FIBRE

FIBRE pulls the sound apart into grains: tiny slices of the recent past, each played back with its own length, pitch and place in the stereo field. Many grains at once make a cloud, from a soft shimmer around the sound to a glittering swarm that has little left of the original.

It keeps listening while it plays, so the cloud follows what you play. Freeze it, and it holds the sound it has and keeps scattering grains from it for as long as you like.

How to play it

  • Turn RATE right of the middle for grains at random, left for a steady pace. In the middle no grains come, only the ones you trigger.
  • Turn SIZE for short, crackling grains or long, smooth ones.
  • Turn PITCH to shift the grains. Turn its L knob to scatter their pitch.
  • Turn POS to reach further back in time. Its L knob scatters where the grains come from.
  • On page 2, SHAPE softens the grains, FDBK feeds them back for a denser cloud, SPRD spreads them wide, MIX sets source against grains.
  • Press A (FRZ) to hold the buffer; the top line blinks FREEZE meanwhile. A gate on GRN (the trigger page) plays one grain, so the grains follow your rhythm.

DRAFT

DRAFT changes the pitch of a sound without changing its speed. It keeps the last four seconds of the track like a tape loop and plays them back in short, overlapping windows, so a voice can sing an octave lower at the same pace, or a chord shift up without hurrying.

How to play it

REED

REED is a bank of 48 resonant bands, tuned to a note and a scale, like the reed of a loom with one thread in every slot. With a short decay it is a filter with a character of its own: a peak that can be gentle or sharp, and a slope that tilts the sound from dark to thin.

Turn the decay up and the bands start to ring on as pitches after every hit. Any sound turns into pitched ringing in the scale you pick: a drum loop becomes a chord, a stream of hits a melody.

How to play it

  • Turn CUT to place the peak on a note, and RESO to make it sharper.
  • Turn SLOPE to let the lows or the highs fall away.
  • Turn DCAY past the middle to make the bands ring. Its L knob on CHOKE stops the ring when the input stops.
  • On page 2, pick the notes in SCALE and move them all with PITCH.
  • For a melody, turn PITCH's L knob: each hit then rings one note of the scale. PENTA and about ±7 is a good start.
  • Press A (HIT) to strike the bands, so they ring with nothing coming in.

TWILL

TWILL is six bells tuned to the notes of a chord or a scale. Only those notes ring, clean and glassy, whatever goes in: a drone turns into a chord, and every hit rings the bells like struck glass. Each bell is two filters on the same note, one that lets only that note in and one that sets how long it rings.

The bells can ring together as a chord or one after another as an arpeggio. They can walk through the chord, follow the key you play, or drift to richer notes of the same chord family on every hit. The tunings go from the piano's to pure intervals and the scales of gamelan and Indian music.

How to play it

  • Pick a chord or scale in SCALE, and the root in KEY. KEY's L knob on MIDI lets the keyboard set the root.
  • Turn DIR for how many bells ring at once. Its L knob sets what each hit does: UP or DOWN walks the bells round, RAND jumps.
  • Turn SPRD to spread the bells wider over the chord. Its L knob picks which bells ring.
  • Turn ROTA to turn the bells through the chord into other voicings. Its L knob (MORPH) blends from one to the next.
  • Turn VARI to move some bells to nearby notes of the chord's family on every hit.
  • Turn DCAY for how long they ring. Low settings let your sound through, shaped by the six notes.
  • Press A (HIT) to strike the bells. Put a TREADLE or a gate on HIT (the trigger page) to play them in rhythm.
More about TWILL

On the weave view TWILL shows as TWL.

TEXTURE

TEXTURE is the dirt and colour of the chain: drive, wavefolding, bit and sample-rate crushing, a tilt EQ, and noise that follows the input's level. HEDDLE

HEDDLE lifts a sound up or pushes it down, as the heddles of a loom lift and lower the threads. As a frequency shifter it moves every frequency by the same number of Hertz, so the harmonics stop fitting together: a plain note turns bell-like and metallic, large shifts clang like a ring modulator, and tiny shifts make a slow, rolling phasing.

As an endless phaser it leaves the pitch alone and sweeps a row of notches up or down through the sound for ever, the barber-pole sweep that never turns round. It works best on rich sounds: pads, noise, chords, distorted sources. HEDDLE goes in TEXTURE's slot instead of TEXTURE.

How to play it

  • In the weave view, turn the L knob on TEXTURE's slot to pick HEDDLE.
  • On the first setting, turn the L knob to pick SHIFT or PHASE, free or on the clock (SHCLK, PHCLK).
  • Turn it right of the middle to go up, left to go down. A tiny amount gives a slow sweep, a big one a clang.
  • For SHIFT, SIDE sets up, down, or BOTH for a wide stereo picture. For PHASE, STAGE sets how many notches.
  • Turn FDBK (SHIFT) for spirals, or RESO (PHASE) for sharp, whistling notches.
  • Turn the L knobs for variations: a new side, stage count or amount on every beat.
  • Press A (NEW) for new variations at once. A gate on FLIP (the trigger page) turns the shift the other way.
More about HEDDLE
  • SHIFT, a frequency shifter after Harald Bode's. Every frequency in the sound moves by the same number of Hertz, up or down. Shift by +100 Hz and 100 Hz becomes 200, 200 becomes 300, 300 becomes 400: the harmonics stop fitting together, so even a plain note turns bell-like and metallic. Large shifts clang like a ring modulator; with FDBK the sound spirals.
  • PHASE, an endless phaser. It leaves the pitch alone and cuts a row of narrow notches into the sound, then slides them up or down for ever: the barber-pole sweep that never turns round. Three phasers run at once, a third of the way apart, each fading in at the bottom and out at the top, so the movement never jumps. It works best on rich sounds: pads, noise, chords, distorted sources.
  • SHCLK and PHCLK are the same two on the clock: the speed is a clock note value instead of Hz or octaves a second.

In short: SHIFT bends the notes, PHASE sweeps the tone. HEDDLE starts with MIX at 100 and SHIFT at 0, which leaves the sound as it is: turn SHIFT away from the middle to hear it.

REPEAT

REPEAT is a tape-style stereo delay: every sound comes back after the delay time, and goes round again as long as the feedback lets it. On each pass the repeats are darkened or thinned, smeared across the stereo field and gently saturated, so a long trail wears down like an old tape loop instead of staying clean. More playback heads can join before the main echo, in one of thirteen patterns, from a dotted eighth or a triplet to a slapback, a clave or the uneven heads of an old tape echo.

Page 2 is a reverb built from eight lines that feed into each other, some of them slowly wobbling so the tail never rings metallic. It reaches from a small room to a sixteen second cloud, with soft, dark walls or hard, bright ones. It can also shimmer: the tail is pitched and sent back into itself, so it climbs with every pass into a halo above the sound, in one of twenty recipes. Turning through the recipes, Loom waits until the knob rests (about a quarter of a second) and then blends into the new one, so the recipes passed on the way don't cut in.

How to play it

  • Turn TIME to space the echoes. Turn its L knob to CLOCK and the time locks to note values of the clock.
  • Turn FDBK up for longer trails: around 50 a few repeats, from 90 up almost endless. Turn its L knob to pick the playback heads: ONE for a plain delay, DOTTD, TRIPL, CLAVE, TAPE and more for a rhythm of echoes.
  • SPRD widens the echoes: PHASE lets the right side lag behind, PING bounces them from side to side, DIFF melts them into a wash.
  • Press A (FRZ) to hold what is in the delay and play over it; the top line blinks FREEZE meanwhile. Press it again to let go.
  • Press B to switch off the part whose page you are on. What is still sounding rings out.
  • On page 2, turn ROOM for the size and the length of the tail, WALL for dark and dense or bright and reflective, and PRE for a gap that keeps the attack clear.
  • For a shimmer, turn ROOM's L knob right and turn GLINT up, then turn WALL's L knob right and pick a preset in PRSET.

CLOTH

CLOTH is the end of every track: what comes off the loom. It sets how loud the track is, where it sits between left and right, and how much of the effects you hear against the dry source. Its picture is a live spectrum analyser of the track's output.

Page 2 is a three-band EQ with a compressor in each band, so the lows, mids and highs can each be lifted, cut or held together on their own. A limiter behind it keeps the tracks from ever clipping. CLOTH is also where you record the whole set to the card.

How to play it

  • Turn MIX from the dry source to the whole chain.
  • Turn LEVEL for the track's volume (100 leaves it as it is) and PAN to place it.
  • Push R on MUTE to fade the track out and back in.
  • On page 2, turn LOW, MID and HIGH to lift or cut each band. Turn their L knobs to squash a band and hold it together.
  • Turn SPLIT to move where the bands divide.
  • Hold A for 1 s to start recording the whole set (a BOLT), and again to stop. A gate on DUCK (the trigger page) dips the track for a moment, like a kick pumping it.

Recording the whole set (BOLT)

Loom can record everything it plays to the SD card, for as long as you like: a whole performance, both tracks after their CLOTH pages, exactly as it leaves the module's output jacks. The quick listen from the file lists is left out, so checking files during a set doesn't end up on the recording.

  • On either track's CLOTH page, hold A for 1 s to start, and hold it again to stop. While you hold, REC (or STOP) fills up at the top of the page. A short press does nothing, so a take can't start or end by accident. It always records both tracks. A gate or a MIDI note can start and stop it too, at once (hold the L knob on the page to pick one).
  • While it records, the weave view shows a blinking dot and the running time in place of the slot name. CLOTH's own header shows the time too, unless the track is muted or bypassed.
  • Each take is saved in LOOM/BOLTS, for example Bolt_S014_02 (see file names below). Takes are stereo, 16-bit, 48 kHz. That is about 11 MB a minute, or 690 MB an hour. A single take stops by itself after about 6 hours.
  • The file is brought up to date every 5 seconds. If the power goes off during a take, everything up to the last few seconds is kept.
  • Opening card mode, or switching away from Loom, ends a running take properly.

File names

Everything Loom saves to the card, a SPOOL loop or a recording of the whole set (a BOLT), is named by session: Bolt_S014_02 is the second thing saved in session 14. A session is one stretch between switching on and off, and it only counts once something is saved in it, so the numbers stay small and have no gaps.

Loops and takes share the count, so everything from one evening sits together, in the order you made it, in LOOM/SPOOLS and LOOM/BOLTS.

How to play it

  • Hold A for 1 s on either CLOTH page to start a recording, and again to stop.
  • Open SHUTTLE's file list and go into BOLTS to find your takes.
  • Press B on a take for a quick listen, push R to load it into SHUTTLE and play it through the chain again.
  • Copy takes to a computer in card mode.
More about File names

Names don't carry a date, because the module has no clock battery and doesn't know the date after it has been switched off.

While it records, BOLT holds back about 1 MB of memory to ride over the moments when the card pauses. SPOOL and SHUTTLE have that much less while a take runs. If the card can't keep up (a slow or very old card), SLOW blinks in place of the time and a little audio is lost. If a take can't start, CLOTH's header says why for 3 seconds: NO CARD or NO MEM.

Modulators

Each track has four modulators of its own, A to D, and both tracks share four more, E to H. A modulator is a source of movement: a wave (WAVE), an envelope (ADSR), a random pattern (RANDOM), a follower of the level (FOLLOW), a drum pattern (TREADLE) or a set of clock dividers (PICK). They move settings, strike devices' actions and come out of the eight CV jacks.

The MOD view shows them as a row of boxes, each drawing what its modulator puts out, newest on the right. Page 1 is the track's own, page 2 the shared ones, page 3 the CV outputs and page 4 the MIDI maps.

  • On a running WAVE, PICK or TREADLE, hold A for 2 s to stop it, press A to run it again, B to start it over.
  • Push L on a box, then turn L, to pick which of a PICK's or TREADLE's outputs it sends.

TREADLE: drum patterns

On a loom, the treadling is the order in which the treadles are pressed: the rhythm of the weave. TREADLE plays drum grooves, each 32 steps (two bars of sixteenths) long, with a low part (bass drum), a mid part (snare) and a high part (hi-hats). All three play at once, each on its own output, as 10 V triggers.

It knows eight styles, from rock and funk to house, breakbeats, latin and dub, four beats each, plus 25 more. Each part can be thinned out or filled in, given its own length to drift against the others, and varied the way a drummer would, with ghost notes, pickups, fills and swing.

How to play it

  • On page 2 (GROOVE), pick a STYLE and a BEAT in it.
  • On page 1 (FILL), turn LOW, MID and HIGH: low plays only the main hits, high fills every step.
  • Turn their L knobs to give a part its own length, so it drifts against the others.
  • Turn CHAOS right for drummer figures, left to thin the groove out. Its L knob sets the swing.
  • Turn PLAY for backwards, back and forth, or drunk.
  • To play drums, send LOW, MID and HIGH to three CV outputs. To play TWILL or BEATER, put the TREADLE's MOD on their HIT.

  • The picture shows the three parts in rows, LOW at the top, each as long as it is: a bar for every step that plays (accents the full row), a dot for the others, and a line under the step playing.

Striking a device. Any modulator can strike a device's actions: on the trigger page (hold L), the sources go on after the jacks and MIDI with MOD A to MOD H. A modulator strikes each time its output rises past half way. Set TWILL's HIT to the TREADLE's MOD and the pattern plays TWILL's bells; ADSR's TRIG and RANDOM's CLOCK can take them too.

A whole kit. Put a TREADLE in a box, then let two more boxes send its MID and HIGH (see Several outputs). On three CV outputs, that plays three drums from one pattern: every hit a 10 V trigger. For accents, send ACC.L, ACC.M or ACC.H to a level on the same drum.

PICK: clock dividers

On a loom, a pick is one pass of the weft; the picks per inch say how dense the cloth is. PICK is a clock with its dividers: RATE ticks, and the dividers each strike on every so-many ticks. Two free dividers, DIVA and DIVB, make rhythms against each other (3 against 4, 7 against 5), and eight fixed ones give the straight and triplet values.

It can thin its ticks out at random, so the rhythm stumbles, and play short bursts when it is struck, a roll per note. Its outputs go to settings, devices' actions and the CV jacks, so one PICK can clock a whole patch.

How to play it

  • Turn RATE for the tick, free or (L knob on CLOCK) a note value.
  • Turn DIVA and DIVB for how many ticks between their hits. Their L knobs keep the count to a set: odd, prime, Fibonacci and so on.
  • Turn DENS down to drop ticks at random. Its L knob on TOCK lets the dividers count only the ticks that get through.
  • On page 2, give START a source to play bursts of BURST ticks. GATE sets how long each hit lasts.
  • Press B to line the dividers up again from the start.
More about PICK
  • DIVA and DIVB each strike every so-many ticks (DIVB can be OFF).
  • Their L knob keeps the count to a set of numbers: ALL (1 to 144), ODD, EVEN, PRIME (2, 3, 5, 7, 11 …), TRIP (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24 … straight and triplet values), FIB (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21 …) or 2^N (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 …). The R knob then steps from one member of the set to the next, and a modulator on the setting only lands on members too. Once a set is chosen, it shows as the setting's name.
  • Rolling counts. A divider changed while running counts on from its last hit rather than starting over. So A and B on the same number can strike on different ticks. RESET, B, or the clock starting lines everything up again.
  • DENS lets each tick through to the TOCK output at random: at 100 all of them, lower and more are left out. Its L knob sets what the dividers count: TICK, every tick, or TOCK, only the ones let through, so the whole rhythm thins out and stumbles.
  • START and BURST: with a START source, PICK rests until it is struck, then plays BURST ticks (1 to 64) and rests again. Struck by a slow WAVE it plays a little roll each time round; struck from a gate input, a burst per note.
  • GATE sets how long each hit lasts, in ms: SHORT (all the way down: a quarter of a tick, 5 to 30 ms), or 1 ms to 1 s, for longer gates on a CV output. A hit never lasts longer than most of a tick, so hits stay apart; at fast rates, GATE shows the length it really plays.
  • Outputs: A+B (A full height, B lower, at 6 V on a CV output), A, B, TICK, TOCK, and the fixed /2, /3, /4, /6, /8, /12, /16 and /24.
  • The picture shows the last 40 ticks going by: A tall, B half height, a dot under every tick, two under the ones let through to TOCK. Between bursts there are no dots, and while a burst plays, the number of ticks left shows in the corner.

Several outputs

PICK and TREADLE have several outputs, while a box in the MOD view sends one. Any other box can send one of their outputs instead of running a modulator of its own, so one TREADLE can play a whole kit on three jacks, and one PICK can clock several devices at different speeds.

How to play it

  • In the MOD view, push L on a box, then turn L: its own outputs come first, then those of the other boxes.
  • Push L again (or press Y) to confirm.
  • Turn L without the push to bring the box's own modulator back.
More about Several outputs
  • First come the outputs of its own modulator. On a PICK box: A+B, A, B, TICK and so on.
  • Then the outputs of the other boxes that have several. The box then runs nothing of its own and sends that output; it shows the box's letter and the output's name, lit on each hit. The bottom line says, for example, from A /4.
  • A to D can take outputs from this track's A to D and from E to H; E to H only from E to H, as they are shared by both tracks.
  • R push on such a box opens the modulator it takes from. Turning L (without the push) brings the box's own modulator back.

Everything that listens to a box gets what it sends: a setting's MOD1 and MOD2, a trigger page, ADSR's TRIG, RANDOM's CLOCK, and the CV outputs. So one PICK can clock several devices, and one TREADLE can play a whole kit.

RANDOM's QUANT: random notes

QUANT puts RANDOM's steps on the notes of a scale. The R knob picks the scale: off, CHROM (all twelve notes), MAJOR, MINOR, PENTA, mPENT (minor pentatonic), BLUES, FIFTH (root and fifth), WHOLE (whole tones), and the microtonal ones also found in TWILL: QUART, 17ET, 19ET, SLEND, PELOG, BOHLN, SHRUT and HARMO. The L knob picks the root note, C to B.

  • RANDOM's whole range is four octaves. Modulate a PITCH setting with it as u at +100 % and every step lands on a note of the scale. With b, use +50 % to stay on the notes.
  • AMNT makes the range smaller, OFFS moves it up. With SMTH up, it glides from note to note.
  • On a CV output, a RANDOM with QUANT on comes out as pitch: 1.2 V per octave on the Buchla-format module, 1 V per octave on the Serge and Eurorack formats. The module knows which one it is, so there is nothing to set. Patch it into an oscillator and it plays in tune, over four octaves from 0 V.

Clock

One clock runs everything in Loom that keeps time: synced delays and modulators, SHUTTLE on SYNC, TREADLE and PICK, the variations that move on every beat. It runs on its own tempo, or follows MIDI clock or pulses at a trigger or CV input from your sequencer.

A small play triangle or stop square in the top line shows whether it runs. Synced modulators hold while it is stopped and start again together with it.

How to play it

  • Press Z to start or stop the clock.
  • Hold Z and let go to open the clock page. Y goes back.
  • Turn BPM for the tempo, or press A (TAP) in time.
  • Set SOURCE to MIDI or a trigger input to follow your sequencer, and PPQN to how many pulses make a beat.
  • Set SOURCE to SHU A or SHU B to follow the sound file playing in that track's SHUTTLE.
  • Press B (RST) to start the bar again.

CV outputs

Eight jacks, A to H, send modulators out to the rest of the rack, 0 to 10 V. A wave becomes an LFO for a filter, an ADSR an envelope, a TREADLE's parts triggers for three drums. A RANDOM with QUANT on comes out as pitch, in tune at the module's own volts per octave.

They live on page 3 of the MOD view. By default A to D carry track A's modulators and E to H track B's.

How to play it

  • Turn R to pick a jack, push R to grab it, then turn R to pick its modulator.
  • Turn L to switch the group: track A, track B, or the shared modulators.
  • Push L to switch a jack off (0 V), and again to bring it back.
More about CV outputs
  • R turn picks the jack. R push grabs it, and then R turn picks the modulator.
  • L turn switches the group: track A, track B, or shared (AB).
  • L push bypasses the jack (0 V). Shown as --.

MIDI maps

Loom has 32 MIDI maps, M1 to M32. Each one listens to one thing from your MIDI controller, a CC knob, notes, velocity or the pitch bend wheel, and turns it into a source like a modulator. A map can move any setting, or press a device's actions on its trigger page.

They live on page 4 of the MOD view. Each map learns its control from your controller, so there is nothing to look up.

How to play it

  • Put the cursor on a setting, push L for MOD1 and hold X, then move a knob on your controller. That's all.
  • Or, on page 4, push R on a map to open it, press A to learn, B to clear.
  • In a map, set CHAN, TYPE and the LOW / HIGH range by hand.
More about MIDI maps
  • R turn moves through the maps. R push opens one.
  • In a map: R turn moves between the fields, R push grabs one, and R turn then changes it. Y steps back.
  • A = learn: the map waits for the next knob you move or key you play on your controller, and takes it.
  • B = clear the map.

Presets & saving

Loom keeps your working settings, the whole set across both tracks, and 30 preset slots to store and recall complete setups. It always starts with the last saved settings, not a preset. You can save the whole state, or just the device you are working on, and leave the rest as it was.

Loom doesn't save by itself, so hold Y before you switch off. Storing and recalling both need a hold, so nothing happens by accident in the middle of a set.

How to play it

  • Hold Y for 2 s in the weave view (or the MOD view) to save everything: SAVE STATE.
  • Hold Y for 2 s on a device's page to save only that device: SAVE DEVICE. Handy when the rest of the set should come back as it was.
  • Push both knobs together (L+R) for the preset page, and turn to pick a slot.
  • Hold the L knob to store into the slot, hold the R knob to recall it. A bar fills while you hold.
More about Presets & saving

The working settings are saved when you hold Y for 2 s (not in a file list, where Y goes up a folder). What is saved depends on where you are. In the weave view, the MOD view, the CV outputs and the MIDI maps it is SAVE STATE: everything, both tracks, every device, the modulators, the clock, the links and where you were. On a device's page (a modulator's and the clock's too) it is SAVE DEVICE: only that device's settings, while everything else stays as it was last saved. The box shows which while you hold, then STATE SAVED or DEVICE SAVED. Both only change what Loom starts with after switching on; the presets are never touched. (If a device has never been saved before, SAVE DEVICE saves everything once instead.) Presets live in LOOM.BIN on the SD card. Loom always starts with the last saved settings, not a preset.

Loom doesn't save by itself. Anything changed since the last save is gone when the power goes off, so hold Y before you switch off. Saving takes a moment in which the sound stops for about an eighth of a second, so save between pieces rather than in the middle of one.

Card mode

Card mode connects the module's SD card to your computer over USB, so you can copy sound files onto it and get your loops and recordings off it, without taking the card out. Loom pauses while it is open, and the module itself shows the card's contents, so you can listen to files and delete them right there.

How to play it

  • Connect the module with a USB cable, then hold Z and push R.
  • Open the card on the computer. On a Mac, use the free app OpenMTP.
  • Copy WAV files into the LOOM folder; SHUTTLE finds them there.
  • On the module, press B to listen to a file, hold X for 2 s to delete it.
  • Hold Z and push R again to go back to Loom.
More about Card mode
  1. Connect the module to the computer with a USB cable.
  2. Hold Z and push R. Loom saves its settings and pauses, and the sound stops.
  3. Open the card on the computer. On Windows and Linux it shows up like a phone or camera. On a Mac you need the free app OpenMTP.
  4. Copy sound files into the LOOM folder (16-bit WAV, mono or stereo). SHUTTLE finds them there. Loops saved from SPOOL are in LOOM/SPOOLS, recordings in LOOM/BOLTS, and single-cycle waveforms for WARP go in LOOM/THRUMS.
  5. When you're done, hold Z and push R again to go back to Loom.
A SPOOL loop is only kept in memory. Opening card mode clears it, so hold A on SPOOL for 2 s to save it to the card first.

While it waits, the page also shows Loom's version and build number, for example Loom Beta 1 c5cbadd8. Until a program on the computer opens the card, the screen says waiting.... On a Mac, if OpenMTP doesn't see the card, the Mac's own photo import may have grabbed it first. The module then says connected anyway. Quit the photo import, then try OpenMTP again.

The module shows the card's contents too, starting in the LOOM folder, with folders first. The small .loom files that hold each sound file's settings are not listed; they stay with their sound file. New files appear within a couple of seconds after you copy them:

  • R turn moves through the list. R push opens a folder.
  • Y goes back up a folder (so does “..” at the top).
  • B listens to the selected sound file, as in SHUTTLE's file list: once through, straight from the card. Loops in SPOOLS play just their loop section, other files play whole. B again stops it. Hold B for AUTO: every file the cursor stops on plays by itself.
  • Hold X for 2 s to delete the selected file. A bar fills while you hold. Let go early and nothing happens. Deleting a sound file also deletes its saved settings.
